Why Ethical Hacking Is the Most In-Demand Skill Now

AK Ankit Kaushik · Lead Trainer, Full Stack & Web Development | Updated 12 September 2026 | 18 min read Free · Demand Analysis

Quick answer — why ethical hacking is in demand

Ethical hacking is the most in-demand skill now because cyberattacks are surging across every industry, regulations require mandatory security testing, and there's a severe shortage of skilled offensive security professionals. This combination has pushed salaries 20–40% above comparable IT roles — and demand keeps growing.

In this analysis you will learn:

  1. Three demand drivers (3 factors) — threats, regulations, and digital transformation.
  2. The talent shortage (3 data points) — why supply can't meet demand.
  3. Salary premium (2 comparisons) — how ethical hacking pay compares to other IT roles.
  4. How to capitalize — the fastest path into the field.

SECTION 01Three Demand Drivers (3 factors)

D1Cyberattacks are surging across every sector

What's happening: Ransomware, phishing, supply chain attacks, and data breaches have increased sharply. Banking, healthcare, government, education, and manufacturing are all being targeted — no sector is safe.

Why it drives demand: Every breach is a wake-up call. Companies that previously ignored security are now hiring ethical hackers to find vulnerabilities before attackers do.

2026 outlook: Attack volume continues to grow, and attackers are using AI to scale their operations — making defensive and offensive security skills more valuable than ever.

D2Regulations mandate security testing

What's happening: RBI guidelines, CERT-In mandates, the DPDP Act, and SEBI regulations now require regular security assessments, vulnerability testing, and incident reporting for many organizations.

Why it drives demand: Compliance isn't optional. Companies must hire ethical hackers to conduct penetration tests, vulnerability assessments, and red team exercises — or face penalties.

2026 outlook: Compliance-driven testing is a stable, growing source of demand, especially in BFSI (banking, financial services, insurance) and healthcare.

D3Digital transformation created new attack surfaces

What's happening: Companies are moving to cloud, adopting APIs, deploying IoT devices, and integrating AI systems — each of these creates new vulnerabilities that need testing.

Why it drives demand: Traditional security testing doesn't cover cloud misconfigurations, API vulnerabilities, or AI-specific attacks. Organizations need ethical hackers who understand these new technologies.

2026 outlook: Cloud security and AI security are among the fastest-growing specializations within ethical hacking — and among the highest-paid.

SECTION 02The Talent Shortage (3 data points)

S11M+ unfilled cybersecurity roles in India

The number: Industry estimates suggest India has over 1 million unfilled cybersecurity positions. Globally, the gap is estimated at 3–4 million professionals.

Why it matters: This isn't a temporary gap — it's structural. The supply of skilled professionals isn't growing fast enough to meet demand.

What it means for you: If you build genuine offensive security skills, you'll be entering a market with far more jobs than qualified candidates.

S2Offensive security shortage is even more severe

The reality: While there's a broad cybersecurity shortage, the gap in offensive security (pentesting, red teaming) is even more acute. Far fewer professionals have hands-on exploitation skills.

Why it matters: Defensive roles (SOC analysts) are more common because the barrier to entry is lower. Offensive roles require deeper technical skills — and there simply aren't enough people who have them.

Salary impact: This scarcity is why penetration testers and red team operators command premium salaries — sometimes 30–50% more than equivalent defensive roles.

S3Employers are hiring at every level — including entry

What's happening: The shortage is so severe that companies are hiring entry-level candidates and training them — something that rarely happened five years ago.

Why it matters: You don't need years of experience to enter the field. With certifications, lab practice, and a portfolio, freshers can get hired.

What employers look for: Certifications (Security+, CEH, OSCP), hands-on lab experience (TryHackMe, HackTheBox), CTF participation, and bug bounty reports.

Bottom line: The talent shortage means the door is open for motivated beginners — but you must prove your skills with labs and projects, not just certificates.

SECTION 03Salary Premium (2 comparisons)

P1Ethical hacking vs. general IT roles

Entry-level: Ethical hacking freshers earn ₹4–8 LPA, compared to ₹3–5 LPA for general IT support or testing roles. That's a 30–60% premium.

Mid-level: Penetration testers with 3–5 years of experience earn ₹12–25 LPA, compared to ₹8–15 LPA for comparable non-security IT roles.

Senior: Red team leads and security consultants earn ₹25–60+ LPA — significantly more than senior developers or testers with the same years of experience.

Why the premium: Scarcity. When there aren't enough skilled people to fill roles, companies must pay more to attract and retain them.

P2Why ethical hacking salaries are growing faster

Growth rate: Cybersecurity salaries in India are growing 8–12% year-over-year, faster than most other IT domains (which average 5–8%).

Why: Demand is growing faster than supply. As regulations tighten and attacks increase, companies must pay more to secure talent.

Specialization premium: Cloud security, AI security, and exploit development skills command even higher premiums — another 20–30% above general ethical hacking roles.

Global remote work: Indian ethical hackers working remotely for US/EU companies can earn significantly more, often in USD — further increasing the earning ceiling.

SECTION 04How to Capitalize

Here's how to take advantage of the ethical hacking demand in 2026:

  • Build fundamentals first: Networking (TCP/IP, DNS, HTTP), Linux, Windows, and a scripting language (Python, Bash). You can't hack what you don't understand.
  • Learn web application security: OWASP Top 10, Burp Suite, and manual testing techniques. Web app pentesting is the most common entry point.
  • Practice in labs: TryHackMe, HackTheBox, PortSwigger Academy, and VulnHub. Document everything — your write-ups become your portfolio.
  • Do CTFs and bug bounties: Capture The Flag competitions and bug bounty programs give real-world practice and evidence of skill.
  • Get one strong certification: eJPT or CEH to start, then OSCP — it's the biggest signal for employers.
  • Build a public presence: Write a blog, post write-ups on Medium, contribute to open source security tools, and be active on LinkedIn.
  • Apply for SOC or junior VAPT roles: Many ethical hackers start in SOC analyst or junior VAPT roles to get professional experience, then move into dedicated pentesting.
Pro tip: The demand is real, but so is the competition for entry-level roles. The candidates who get hired aren't the ones with the most certifications — they're the ones with the strongest hands-on proof. Labs, CTFs, and write-ups beat certificates alone.

SECTION 06Frequently asked questions

Why is ethical hacking in such high demand in 2026?

Three factors: rising cyberattacks across every sector, regulations requiring mandatory security testing, and a severe talent shortage. India alone has over 1 million unfilled cybersecurity roles, with offensive security being the most acute gap.

How much more do ethical hackers earn compared to other IT roles?

Ethical hackers typically earn 20–40% more than comparable non-security IT roles at the same experience level. At senior levels, red team leads and security consultants can earn significantly more than senior developers.

Is the demand for ethical hackers going to last?

Yes — the demand is structural, not cyclical. As digital transformation continues, regulations tighten, and attacks increase, the need for skilled ethical hackers will keep growing. This isn't a temporary trend.

Can I get into ethical hacking without experience?

Yes. The talent shortage means companies are hiring entry-level candidates who can demonstrate skills through certifications, hands-on labs, CTFs, and bug bounty reports. Your portfolio matters more than years of experience.
