Layoff Recovery · First 30 Days

What I Would Do in the First 30 Days After a Layoff

AK Ankit Kaushik · Lead Trainer, Data Engineering & AI | Updated 28 August 2026 | 16 min read Free · Career Guide

Quick summary — the first 30 days after a layoff

The first 30 days aren't about applying to everything — they're about getting steady. This guide breaks down exactly what to do each day, week, and phase so you leave day 30 with a plan, not just a pile of tabs.

In this guide you will learn:

  1. Days 1-10: Process & Stabilize — daily and weekly plan.
  2. Days 11-20: Reassess & Rebuild — daily and weekly plan.
  3. Days 21-30: Reconnect & Prepare — daily and weekly plan.
  4. Tracking templates — track your progress.
  5. Accountability tips — stay grounded.

SECTION 01Days 1-10: Process & Stabilize

The first ten days aren't about job hunting — they're about not making decisions from a place of panic. Here's your plan:

Days Focus Daily Actions Goal by end
Days 1-2 Let It Land Process emotions, avoid big decisions, rest Feel steady enough to plan
Days 3-4 Know Your Numbers Check severance, benefits, unemployment options, runway Know your financial runway
Days 5-7 Tell Your Network Message close contacts, update close family/friends Your circle knows you're looking
Days 8-10 Build a Routine Set wake time, workspace, daily structure A repeatable daily routine 
Days 1-10 — Daily Schedule:
7:30 AM — Wake up, light exercise (20-30 min)
8:30 AM — Breakfast, no phone
9:30 AM — One focused task for the day
   - Days 1-2: Rest, process, journal
   - Days 3-4: Review finances and benefits
   - Days 5-7: Reach out to your network
   - Days 8-10: Set up your daily routine
12:00 PM — Lunch break
1:00 PM — Admin (unemployment, benefits, paperwork)
3:00 PM — Walk or break
4:00 PM — Light reading / research
6:00 PM — Time with family or friends
9:00 PM — Wind down, no job boards after 8 PM
Week 1-2 Review:
□ Have I given myself time to process, not just react?
□ Do I know my financial runway in months?
□ Have I checked severance and benefits details?
□ Have I told my close network I'm looking?
□ Do I have a daily routine that isn't collapsing?
□ Have I avoided major financial or career decisions made in panic?
days1-10-plan.md
Key insight: Applying to jobs on day 1 usually produces weak applications. Give yourself the first ten days to stabilize — the search will be stronger for it.

SECTION 02Days 11-20: Reassess & Rebuild

The second phase is about reassessing your direction and rebuilding your materials. Here's your plan:

Days Focus Daily Actions Goal by end
Days 11-13 Reassess Direction Decide: same role, adjacent role, or pivot A clear target role or two
Days 14-16 Rebuild Resume Rewrite with impact statements and numbers A resume draft you'd send today
Days 17-18 Refresh LinkedIn Update headline, summary, "Open to Work" A complete, current profile
Days 19-20 Identify Gaps List skills or proof points you're missing A short list of what to build next 
Days 11-20 — Daily Schedule:
7:30 AM — Wake up, exercise (30 min)
8:30 AM — Review goals for the day
9:30 AM — Deep work (2-3 hours)
   - Days 11-13: Reassess target roles
   - Days 14-16: Resume rewrite
   - Days 17-18: LinkedIn refresh
   - Days 19-20: Gap analysis
12:00 PM — Lunch break
1:00 PM — Light networking (comment, message, catch up)
2:30 PM — Deep work (1-2 hours)
5:00 PM — Review progress, plan tomorrow
6:00 PM — Exercise, rest
8:00 PM — Light reading on target roles
Week 2-3 Review:
□ Do I have one or two clear target roles?
□ Is my resume rewritten with real numbers and outcomes?
□ Does it address any career gap directly?
□ Is my LinkedIn headline and summary current?
□ Is "Open to Work" turned on (if I want it visible)?
□ Do I know exactly what skills or proof points I'm missing?
days11-20-plan.md
Key insight: Don't rebuild your resume before you've reassessed direction — a great resume for the wrong role still won't land interviews.

SECTION 03Days 21-30: Reconnect & Prepare

The final phase of the first month is about reconnecting with your network and getting ready to search seriously. Here's your plan:

Days Focus Daily Actions Goal by end
Days 21-23 Reconnect Network Reach out to 3-5 former colleagues daily 15+ real conversations restarted
Days 24-26 Research Companies Build a shortlist of 15-20 target companies A researched target list
Days 27-28 Practice Your Story Draft and rehearse your "what happened" narrative A calm, confident layoff story
Days 29-30 Set Month 2 Plan Decide skills, projects, and application pace A written plan for the next 30 days 
Days 21-30 — Daily Schedule:
7:30 AM — Wake up, exercise (30 min)
8:30 AM — Review goals for the day
9:30 AM — Deep work (2-3 hours)
   - Days 21-23: Networking outreach
   - Days 24-26: Company research
   - Days 27-28: Story and narrative practice
   - Days 29-30: Plan the next 30 days
12:00 PM — Lunch break
1:00 PM — Networking calls or coffee chats
2:30 PM — Deep work (1-2 hours)
5:00 PM — Review progress, plan tomorrow
6:00 PM — Exercise, rest
8:00 PM — Light interview prep (30 min)
Week 3-4 Review:
□ Have I reconnected with 15+ people from my network?
□ Do I have a researched list of target companies?
□ Can I explain the layoff calmly, in under 30 seconds?
□ Do I have a written plan for days 31-60?
□ Am I mentally ready to start applying?
□ Have I protected time to rest, not just grind?
days21-30-plan.md
Key insight: Day 30 isn't a finish line — it's the point where you switch from "getting ready" to "actively searching." Leave this phase with a plan, not just momentum.

SECTION 05Accountability tips

Here are tips to stay grounded during your first 30 days:

  • Find an accountability partner — share your weekly goals with someone who will check in.
  • Track daily wins — write down 3 wins each day, no matter how small.
  • Join a community — connect with others going through the same thing.
  • Protect your routine — a steady wake time and workspace matters more than intensity.
  • Review weekly — every Sunday, review your progress and plan the week ahead.
Pro tip: The goal of the first 30 days isn't job offers — it's a stable routine, a rebuilt resume, and a reconnected network. Everything else follows from that.

SECTION 06Interview Q&A — first 30 days

Q1Should I start applying to jobs on day 1?

Generally no. Applications sent from a place of panic tend to be weaker. Use the first 10 days to stabilize before you start applying seriously.

Q2What if I don't complete a day's tasks?

Don't worry — just pick up where you left off. This plan is a guide, not a strict deadline. Consistency over perfection.

Q3What if I'm already financially stable?

You can spend less time on days 3-4 and use the extra time to go deeper on reassessing direction and rebuilding your resume.

Q4How do I stay motivated in the first month?

Review your progress weekly. Celebrate small wins. Connect with others. Remember you're building a foundation, not racing a clock.

Q5What comes after day 30?

By day 30 you should have a plan for skills, projects, and applications for the next stretch — see our full 90-day planner for what comes next.

SECTION 08Frequently asked questions

What's the most important thing to do in the first week?

Give yourself room to process, then check your financial runway. Big decisions and job applications can wait a few days.

How much time should I commit daily?

2-3 hours of focused work daily is enough for the first 30 days. This phase is about direction and foundation, not volume.

Should I tell people I was laid off?

Yes — telling your close network early is one of the highest-leverage things you can do. Most opportunities come through people, not job boards.

What if I want to change career direction?

Days 11-13 are built for exactly that — use them to reassess before you rebuild your resume around the wrong target.

What should I do after day 30?

Move into upskilling, project building, and structured applications — our full 90-day planner picks up exactly where this leaves off.
