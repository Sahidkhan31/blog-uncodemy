Uncodemy Cloud Engineer Course Review: Worth the Money?

AK Ankit Kaushik · Lead Trainer, Data Engineering & AI | Updated 28 August 2026 | 14 min read Free · Course Review

Quick summary — is the Uncodemy cloud course worth it?

The Uncodemy Cloud Computing course costs ₹25,000 for a 3-month program. It covers AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud fundamentals with hands-on projects and placement support. For ₹25,000, it's a low-risk entry point — but you should visit the center, speak to recent alumni, and verify the curriculum before enrolling.

In this guide, you will learn:

  1. Course overview — duration, fees, mode, and location.
  2. Curriculum — what you actually learn.
  3. Faculty — who teaches and whether credentials are verifiable.
  4. Placement support — what's claimed vs what's proven.
  5. Reviews — what learners and employees say.
  6. Verdict — is it worth the money?

SECTION 01Course overview and fees

Uncodemy is an IT training institute based in Noida, Delhi NCR, offering both online and offline courses across full-stack development, data science, software testing, cloud computing, and more. The cloud computing course is positioned as a bootcamp-style program focused on practical skills.

Detail Information
Course Name Cloud Computing Training (AWS, Azure, GCP)
Duration Up to 3 months
Fees Starting from ₹25,000 (additional discounts available)
Mode Online and offline
Location B 14-15, Udhyog Marg, Sector 1, Noida
Curriculum Focus Cloud architecture, service models, AWS/Azure/GCP, deployment, security, hands-on labs

How it compares to alternatives:

  • Udemy cloud courses: ₹3,000–₹5,000 for self-paced video content
  • IILM University B.Tech Cloud Computing: ₹7.36 lakh–₹9 lakh for a 4-year degree
  • Galgotias University B.Tech Cloud Computing: ₹1.64 lakh per year (4-year degree)

At ₹25,000, Uncodemy sits in the affordable bootcamp tier — more structured than a Udemy course, far cheaper than a degree program.

Key insight: ₹25,000 is a low-risk entry point. Even if the course is mediocre, you're not losing a fortune. The real risk is time — 3 months spent on training that may not lead to a cloud engineer role if placement support doesn't deliver.

SECTION 02Curriculum: what you learn

Uncodemy's cloud computing curriculum covers the standard foundations expected of a cloud engineer:

  • Introduction to Cloud Computing — history, trends, and core concepts
  • Cloud Architectures — public, private, and hybrid models
  • Service Models — IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS
  • Cloud Platforms — AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform
  • Deploying and Managing Applications — automation, scalability, security
  • Cloud Security and Compliance — data security, privacy, regulatory considerations
  • Best Practices — designing, deploying, and managing cloud applications
  • Hands-on Experience — practical work with cloud platforms and tools

The curriculum is broad but standard. It covers the fundamentals well, but does not appear to go deep into advanced topics like Kubernetes, Terraform, or CI/CD pipelines — which are increasingly expected for cloud engineer roles.

Pro tip: Ask for the detailed syllabus before enrolling. If Kubernetes, Terraform, and CI/CD are not included, plan to learn them separately.

SECTION 03Faculty: who teaches

Uncodemy claims its trainers come from Fortune 100 companies and rapidly growing startups. The institute emphasizes "industry oracle faculties" and trainers with real-world experience at MNCs.

However, no specific trainer names, credentials, or LinkedIn profiles are publicly listed for the cloud course. This is a significant gap — you cannot verify who actually teaches the program before enrolling.

For comparison, Udacity's Cloud DevOps Nanodegree publicly lists four instructors with names, roles, and company backgrounds. Uncodemy should provide the same transparency.

Key insight: Ask for the trainer's name and LinkedIn profile before enrolling. If they hesitate, that's a red flag.

SECTION 04Placement support: what's claimed

Uncodemy advertises 100% placement support across its courses. On LinkedIn, the company regularly posts placement announcements:

  • Namesh Joshi — QA Engineer at Tech Devise IT Services, 10.8 LPA
  • Sandhya — Quality Engineer at VEE Healthtek, 8 LPA
  • Highest package of 2026: 26 LPA (role unspecified)

Important caveat: These placements are for software testing and QA roles, not cloud engineering. The LinkedIn posts do not show a single verified cloud engineer placement.

Uncodemy's placement support includes resume building, mock interviews, and job referrals. But without cloud-specific placement data, it's hard to assess how effective the support is for cloud roles specifically.

Pro tip: Ask for cloud-specific placement data — not QA, not testing. How many students got cloud engineer roles? At what companies? What packages?

SECTION 05Reviews: what learners say

Uncodemy's overall rating on AmbitionBox is 4.0/5 from 45+ employee reviews. However, these are employee reviews, not student reviews — a critical distinction.

The eLearning Industry directory shows one detailed review with a 90% overall rating:

  • Likes: Practical approach, hands-on coding sessions, live projects, interview preparation, supportive environment
  • Dislikes: Fast-paced class schedules for advanced topics, wanted more in-depth project sessions

The red flags:

Not all reviews are positive. Some employee reviews on AmbitionBox raise serious concerns:

  • "Worst management... Stress working environment... No fixed working time... Fake commitments to students"
  • "They do not provide you your salary and your documents after you resign... Exploiting their employees"
  • "Company culture is good before but now it's worst"

These are employee complaints, not student complaints. But when a company has employee reviews mentioning "fake commitments to students," it warrants caution. If the staff feel exploited, the training quality may suffer.

There are no independent, verified student reviews for the cloud computing course specifically. This is the biggest gap in assessing whether the course is worth it.

Key insight: The lack of verified student reviews for the cloud course is the biggest unknown. Do your own due diligence — ask for alumni contacts.

Verdict: Is it worth ₹25,000?

It depends on what you're comparing it to.

If your alternative is a ₹3,000 Udemy course, Uncodemy offers more structure, live instruction, and placement support for ₹22,000 more. Whether that's worth it depends on whether you need accountability and career help.

If your alternative is a ₹2 lakh+ degree or premium bootcamp, Uncodemy is a low-cost entry point that covers the fundamentals.

For ₹25,000, the financial risk is low. Even if the course is mediocre, you're not losing a fortune. The real risk is time — 3 months spent on training that may not lead to a cloud engineer role if placement support doesn't deliver.

What to do before enrolling:

  1. Visit the center — Go to the Noida office. Sit in a demo class (Uncodemy offers free demos).
  2. Ask for trainer credentials — Request the name and LinkedIn profile of the cloud course instructor.
  3. Ask for cloud-specific placement data — Not QA, not testing. How many students got cloud engineer roles? At what companies? What packages?
  4. Speak to recent alumni — Ask for contact details of 2–3 students who completed the cloud course. If they hesitate, that's a red flag.
  5. Compare with free alternatives — AWS and Google offer free tier accounts. You can learn the fundamentals yourself. The ₹25,000 buys structure and placement support, not content.
Pro tip: Uncodemy's cloud course is cheap enough to try. But the lack of verified student reviews and cloud-specific placement data means you should do your due diligence before paying.
